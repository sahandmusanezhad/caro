WHY THE GATE REFUSES — the arithmetic under 0.206
==============================================================================

1. THE TWO ESTIMATORS ARE THE SAME ESTIMATOR HERE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  comparable vs global predictions identical   True   (max |diff| 0)
  trims    train  75   test  25   overlap 0
  parents  train  57   test  21   overlap 6

  The split holds out whole TRIMS, so no test row has a comparable
  in training. The ladder falls through to `global` on every row,
  and a comparables estimator with no comparables IS the global
  estimator. Under this split the gate cannot tell them apart —
  and it did not: both reported the same 0.206.

2. THE HELD-OUT TRIMS ARE A DIFFERENT PRICE POPULATION
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  train  n= 177  median    2,300,000,000  p15     876,000,000  p85   6,772,000,000  spread  143x
  test   n=  51  median    3,200,000,000  p15   1,440,000,000  p85   7,485,000,000  spread   53x

  share of TEST rows below TRAIN p15      0.000
  share of TEST rows below TRAIN median   0.294

3. THOSE TWO NUMBERS *ARE* THE COVERAGE FAILURE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  tau 0.15   empirical 0.000   err -0.150   CI [0.00, 0.07]   nominal OUTSIDE 95% CI
  tau 0.35   empirical 0.275   err -0.075   CI [0.17, 0.41]
  tau 0.50   empirical 0.294   err -0.206   CI [0.19, 0.43]   nominal OUTSIDE 95% CI
  tau 0.85   empirical 0.824   err -0.026   CI [0.70, 0.90]

  worst error 0.206, and past 2.5 SE it is still 0.206

  Read them together: 0.000 coverage at tau=0.15 is the same fact
  as 'no test row is cheaper than the train p15', and 0.294 at the
  median is the same fact as 'only 29% of test rows are below the
  train median'. The coverage failure is not a property of the
  estimator. It is what happens when a distribution fitted on one
  price population is asked to be calibrated on another.

WHAT THIS CHANGES, AND WHAT IT DOES NOT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Corrects D41. 'The baseline class does not clear the gate either'
  reads as a fact about the baseline, and it is not: under this
  split EVERY estimator is the same estimator, and it is being
  scored against trims it has never seen at prices it has never
  seen. The refusal is real and correct — CARO still may not serve
  an estimate here — but the reason is the corpus and the split,
  not the model.

  Does NOT rescue the estimator. Run 5's REJECT stands as
  registered, with D39's interval attached, and nothing here is
  evidence that partial pooling would have passed under a kinder
  split. Choosing a kinder split now, after seeing this, is exactly
  the D35 move — so the split is untouched and this is a finding.

  It is the third appearance of D38's blind spot: a registration
  that constrained trim COUNTS and said nothing about price
  heterogeneity. First it decided the benchmark; then it explained
  the thin-slice tension; now it turns out to have decided whether
  a market estimate can be served at all.
