Q2 — SHALLOW YIELD.  Answered, and it corrected a measurement error first.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  The JSON-LD ItemList on a trim page is CAPPED AT 5.

  This was caught by testing it against slugs that cannot possibly hold five:

      /car/pride         →  ItemList reports 5     (the entire Pride inventory)
      /car/peugeot       →  ItemList reports 5
      /car/pride-131     →  ItemList reports 5

  Eight trim pages had already been read this way, and five of them "had
  exactly 5 listings". That number was the cap. Reading trim sizes from it
  would have put a structural artefact of the page into the corpus as a fact
  about the market — the same error class as Run 1's /car/saipa and D31's
  -page-N redirect, now on a third route.

  Corrected method: count distinct detail slugs in the rendered HTML.

      trim slug                  listings    note
      -------------------------------------------------------------
      pride-131-tl                      1
      tiba-hatchback-plus               3
      saina-manuals-mtcng               8
      tiba-sedan-sxcng                  9
      quick-manualrplus-at             10
      pride-141-basic                  30    page cap
      pride-151-gx                     30    page cap
      quick-gx-lmt                     30    page cap
      dena-plus                        30    page cap
      peugeot-206sd-v8                 30    page cap

  A trim page returns at most 30, so 30 means "at least 30". That is
  sufficient — the shape needs well-observed trims at 5 or more, not at 30.


Q4 — SHAPE REACHABILITY.  Answered: yes, the tail is real.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Trims holding 1, 3, 8, 9 and 10 listings were all found in a sample of 12.
  D37's mixed shape needs roughly 15 trims under the floor to supply 58 thin
  rows; trims of that size exist and are enumerable in advance.

  BUT — 2 of the 12 sampled slugs served a GENERIC FEED, not the trim:

      tara-v1          32 listings, 0 on-trim, first item a Hyundai Santa Fe
      renault-l90-e2   32 listings, 0 on-trim, first item the same car

  17% of sampled trim slugs (2/12; the interval on that is wide, roughly
  5-45%). A collector that trusted the slug would have entered 64 unrelated
  cars as Tara V1 and Renault L90 E2 observations — corrupting precisely the
  trim conditioning the whole experiment exists to test, in a way no error
  metric would show, because the corrupted rows sit on both sides of the
  split.

  It is cleanly detectable: on a real trim page every detail slug contains the
  trim slug; on a contaminated one, none do. Run 5's collector MUST carry that
  check, and must count a contaminated page as a dead slug rather than as a
  trim with 32 listings.


Q3 — TAIL CONVERSION.  Answered weakly, and stated weakly.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  7 detail pages from one thin trim (saina-manuals-mtcng):
  6 carried price, odometer and year; 1 carried none of the three.

  6/7 is not evidence that the tail converts better than Run 3's 0.701. At
  n=7 the interval spans roughly half the possible range, and one trim is one
  trim. The only defensible reading is the negative one: nothing here suggests
  the tail converts WORSE, so 0.701 is not knowingly optimistic. It is not
  refined, and §10 keeps it.

  One observation, recorded and NOT acted on: a listing showed 1 km on a 1403
  car. classify_mileage only applies its rate check from three years old, so
  that row is PLAUSIBLE today. It may be a real gap. Changing eligibility now
  is a §4 change that would end Run 5, so it is written down and left alone.
